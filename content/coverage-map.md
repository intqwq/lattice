# Coverage map: a zero-start route toward systematic study

Snapshot: 28 September 2026. This audit describes the authored files at this point in construction. It is a coverage plan, not a diploma-equivalence claim, examination accreditation, or a claim that the learner has mastered the material. Read the generated catalogue for current counts after later authoring batches.

The current pack contains **55 original bilingual lessons and 119 practice items across 42 module IDs**: 25 mathematics/shared-foundation lessons, 18 science lessons, and 12 computing lessons. A module with one or more lessons is **partially authored** unless every declared concept and assessment requirement has been checked. The 90-course, 540-module map is a route map; its node count is not a count of finished teaching units. `module-outlines.mjs` adds **522 specific bilingual concept targets and 174 exit checks across 174 modules in 29 courses**. Those checklists do not themselves teach the concepts.

“Zero start” here means no prior subject knowledge. The app still assumes the learner can read the selected language, identify written symbols, and use a keyboard or pointer. It does not currently teach literacy, provide audio-first instruction, or establish that every beginner can progress without assistance. Arithmetic begins with counting and place value, but the rest of the foundation sequence still needs a stronger variety of examples, misconceptions, independent exercises, and delayed checks.

For example, the present **C02.01** lesson covers basic atomic structure; the full module also calls for spectra and electronic configurations. The **M01.02** lessons do not by themselves certify every linear/quadratic modelling objective. Every published module needs the concept-by-concept check below.

## Per-module publication gate

A module may be called fully authored only when its declared checklist is mapped to actual lesson sections and assessed tasks. Each concept must have an accessible definition, notation and restrictions; a reason or mechanism; at least one worked example; an error/nonexample where useful; unassisted practice; authored feedback and solutions; and a retrieval or transfer check. Beginner prerequisites must be named at concept/lesson granularity and have a usable route back to them.

The editor should record separately:

- **Scope present:** a concrete concept target exists in the outline.
- **Teaching authored:** precise English and Chinese explanations and examples exist.
- **Practice authored:** concept-aligned independent questions, hints and solutions exist.
- **Editorial review:** subject correctness, prerequisite completeness, translation alignment and accessibility have been reviewed.
- **Learner evidence:** an attempt was assisted, independent or self-assessed; later retention is a separate observation.

An outline, an open lesson, a read-completion tick, a correct multiple-choice answer and an independently justified solution are different evidence. The interface must preserve those distinctions. There is no defensible global “diploma percentage” based on lesson count because modules differ in scope and many practical and reasoning outcomes are not automatically checkable.

## Cross-subject completeness checks

- **Kinematics and calculus:** position and average rate → limit and derivative → instantaneous velocity/acceleration. Algebra-based constant-acceleration mechanics remains available before calculus. Calculus adds generality; it is not a prerequisite for every mechanics lesson.
- **Work and integration:** force/displacement and vector projection → signed accumulation and definite integrals → variable-force work. A constant-force worked example remains an earlier entry point.
- **Electricity and algebra:** ratios, units and linear equations → resistance and Kirchhoff circuit equations. Exponentials, derivatives and differential equations enter the later transient models where they are actually used.
- **Chemistry and mathematics:** fractions/ratios → mole ratios → limiting reagents/concentration. Powers/logarithms support pH; conservation and equation-solving support equilibrium; exponential change supports selected kinetics.
- **Biology and chemistry:** particles, bonding, polarity and gradients → membranes, macromolecules and metabolism. Probability and conditional reasoning support genetic inference; graphs/statistics support experimental claims.
- **OI and mathematics:** logic and induction support correctness; counting and modular arithmetic support algorithmic number theory; functions and growth rates support complexity; recurrence thinking connects sequences and dynamic programming.

Cross-subject links must point to the exact needed concept and say why it is needed. A related-interest edge is not a hard prerequisite. A prerequisite graph being acyclic proves only a structural property; it does not show that every pedagogical prerequisite has been discovered.

After each authoring batch, regenerate counts and re-audit concept mapping, English/Chinese alignment, prerequisite closure, question diversity, feedback accuracy and practical-work limitations. A country-independent school-core target should publish a clear outcome framework and map it to more than one reference curriculum before claiming comparable breadth. A formal diploma or external competition qualification remains a separate institution's determination.
